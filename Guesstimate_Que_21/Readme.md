# Guesstimate 21 — Number of Fish in the World's Seas

## Clarifying Questions

1. Are all oceans and seas across the world included?
2. Are all species of fish included?
3. Are rivers, lakes, and ponds excluded?
4. Are we estimating the total fish population at a given point in time?

## Final Question

Estimate the total number of fish of all species currently present across all oceans and seas in the world.

## Solution

### Key Logic

I will estimate the number of fish using:

**Earth's surface area → Water-covered area → Ocean volume → Divide water into depth zones → Apply fish density to each zone → Sum the fish population**

I am using a depth-based approach rather than applying one fish density to the entire ocean because fish are not distributed uniformly throughout the ocean. Areas closer to the surface generally have greater availability of sunlight and food, while fish density decreases as we move into deeper waters.

---

### Step 1: Estimate Earth's Water Surface Area

Approximate surface area of Earth:

**500 million km²**

Assumption:

**70% of Earth's surface is covered by water**

I use 70% because the majority of Earth's surface is covered by oceans and seas.

Therefore:

**Water surface area = 500 million × 70%**

**= 350 million km²**

---

### Step 2: Estimate Ocean Volume

Assumption:

**Average ocean depth ≈ 3.5 km**

I use 3.5 km as an approximate average depth because ocean depth varies significantly across different regions, but using an average depth keeps the guesstimate simple and avoids unnecessary complexity.

Therefore:

**Ocean volume = Surface area × Average depth**

**= 350 million × 3.5**

**≈ 1.225 billion km³**

---

### Step 3: Convert Ocean Volume into Cubic Meters

Since:

**1 km³ = 10⁹ m³**

Therefore:

**1.225 billion km³ = 1.225 × 10¹⁸ m³**

So, estimated ocean volume:

**≈ 1.225 × 10¹⁸ m³**

---

### Step 4: Divide the Ocean into Depth Zones

I do not want to assume the same fish density throughout the entire ocean because fish availability varies with depth.

I divide the ocean into three broad zones:

| Depth Zone | Approx. Share of Ocean Volume | Assumed Fish Density |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0–200 m | 6% | 2 fish/m³ |
| 200–1,000 m | 23% | 0.5 fish/m³ |
| >1,000 m | 71% | 0.05 fish/m³ |

These are simplified assumptions for the guesstimate.

**Why these assumptions?**

- **0–200 m:** Higher fish density because sunlight supports marine food chains and food availability is relatively high.
- **200–1,000 m:** Fish density decreases as sunlight and food availability decline.
- **>1,000 m:** Very little sunlight reaches these depths and food availability is much lower, so I assume a significantly lower fish density.

The percentages sum to approximately 100%, allowing me to distribute the total ocean volume across the three zones.

---

### Step 5: Estimate Fish in Each Zone

#### Zone 1: 0–200 m

Volume:

**1.225 × 10¹⁸ × 6%**

**= 7.35 × 10¹⁶ m³**

At 2 fish/m³:

**Fish = 7.35 × 10¹⁶ × 2**

**= 1.47 × 10¹⁷ fish**

---

#### Zone 2: 200–1,000 m

Volume:

**1.225 × 10¹⁸ × 23%**

**= 2.8175 × 10¹⁷ m³**

At 0.5 fish/m³:

**Fish = 2.8175 × 10¹⁷ × 0.5**

**= 1.40875 × 10¹⁷ fish**

---

#### Zone 3: >1,000 m

Volume:

**1.225 × 10¹⁸ × 71%**

**= 8.6975 × 10¹⁷ m³**

At 0.05 fish/m³:

**Fish = 8.6975 × 10¹⁷ × 0.05**

**= 4.34875 × 10¹⁶ fish**

---

### Step 6: Calculate Total Number of Fish

Total fish:

**= Fish in Zone 1 + Fish in Zone 2 + Fish in Zone 3**

**= 1.47 × 10¹⁷ + 1.40875 × 10¹⁷ + 4.34875 × 10¹⁶**

**≈ 3.31 × 10¹⁷ fish**

For an interview-friendly final estimate, I would round this to:

# **≈ 3 × 10¹⁷ fish**

Or approximately:

**300 quadrillion fish**

---

## Final Estimate

**The estimated number of fish in the world's oceans and seas is approximately 3 × 10¹⁷, or 300 quadrillion fish.**

## Key Assumptions

- Earth's surface area ≈ **500 million km²**
- Approximately **70%** of Earth's surface is covered by water
- Average ocean depth ≈ **3.5 km**
- Fish density is **higher near the surface** and decreases with depth
- All fish species are included
- All life stages are included implicitly because we are estimating the total fish population
- Rivers, lakes, and ponds are excluded
- Mortality is not considered because this is a **point-in-time population estimate**, not a population-growth calculation
- Fish density figures are simplified assumptions used to make the guesstimate feasible
