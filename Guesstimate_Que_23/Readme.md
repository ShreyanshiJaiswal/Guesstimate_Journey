# Guesstimate 23: How Many People Wear Red on a Given Day in Delhi?

## Question

Estimate how many people wear red on a given day in Delhi.

---

## Clarifying Questions

- Which city are we considering — Tier 1, Tier 2, Tier 3, or overall India?
- Is it a normal day or a special occasion such as Valentine's Day, Navratri or Karva Chauth?
- Should cultural and religious influences be considered?
- Should professional uniforms be considered?
- What season/weather conditions are we considering?
- Does the nature of people's work and environment affect the estimate?
- Are we estimating people wearing red at a specific point in time or at any point during the day?

---

## Final Question

**How many people in Delhi wear at least one red clothing item at some point during a given day?**

---

# Solution

## Step 1: Delhi Population

I am taking Delhi's population as approximately **20 million** as a rounded estimate.

---

## Step 2: Population Segmentation

Since Delhi is predominantly urban, I am not using an urban/rural split. Instead, I am segmenting the population based on age and daily activity.

| Population Group | Share | Population |
|---|---:|---:|
| Students / Young Population | 25% | 5M |
| Working Population | 50% | 10M |
| Homemakers / Others | 20% | 4M |
| Elderly | 5% | 1M |
| **Total** | **100%** | **20M** |

**Reason for assumption:** I am using broad segments because clothing choices are likely to differ based on age, lifestyle and work environment.

---

## Step 3: Normal-Day Probability of Wearing Red

| Population Group | Population | % Wearing Red | Red Wearers |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Students / Young Population | 5M | 12% | 0.60M |
| Working Population | 10M | 8% | 0.80M |
| Homemakers / Others | 4M | 10% | 0.40M |
| Elderly | 1M | 5% | 0.05M |
| **Total** | **20M** | | **1.85M** |

### Reasons for Assumptions

- **Students / Young – 12%:** Higher exposure to fashion trends and greater flexibility in clothing choices.
- **Working – 8%:** Lower probability due to formal and workplace-specific dress codes.
- **Homemakers / Others – 10%:** More flexibility in clothing choices, so I keep this close to the overall average.
- **Elderly – 5%:** Lower influence of fashion trends compared with younger groups.

### Calculation

- Students / Young: **5M × 12% = 0.60M**
- Working: **10M × 8% = 0.80M**
- Homemakers / Others: **4M × 10% = 0.40M**
- Elderly: **1M × 5% = 0.05M**

**Total = 0.60M + 0.80M + 0.40M + 0.05M = 1.85M**

---

## Step 4: Professional Requirements

I am already including professional requirements within the **8% assumption for the working population**, rather than adding them separately.

This avoids double-counting people who are already included in the normal-day estimate.

---

## Step 5: Special Occasion Adjustment

For occasions where red is more relevant, I increase the probability of wearing red rather than adding a separate category.

### Valentine's Day

| Population Group | Population | % Wearing Red | Red Wearers |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Students / Young Population | 5M | 20% | 1.00M |
| Working Population | 10M | 12% | 1.20M |
| Homemakers / Others | 4M | 12% | 0.48M |
| Elderly | 1M | 6% | 0.06M |
| **Total** | **20M** | | **2.74M** |

**Reason for assumption:** Red has a strong association with Valentine's Day, so I increase the probability, particularly among younger and working populations.

### Calculation

- Students / Young: **5M × 20% = 1.00M**
- Working: **10M × 12% = 1.20M**
- Homemakers / Others: **4M × 12% = 0.48M**
- Elderly: **1M × 6% = 0.06M**

**Total = 1.00M + 1.20M + 0.48M + 0.06M = 2.74M**

---

# Final Estimate

### Normal Day

**≈ 1.85 million → ~2 million people**

### Valentine's Day

**≈ 2.74 million → ~3 million people**

Therefore, my estimate is:

> **~2 million people wear red on a normal day in Delhi.**

> **~3 million people may wear red on Valentine's Day.**

---

## Key Logic

**Delhi Population → Population Segmentation → Probability of Wearing Red → Special Occasion Adjustment → Final Estimate**

The key influencing factors are **fashion, age, profession, cultural/religious occasions, season and weather**. I have incorporated these factors into the probabilities rather than adding them separately, to avoid double-counting.
