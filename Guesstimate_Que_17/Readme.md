# Guesstimate 17: Number of Bricks in a Building

## Question

Estimate the number of bricks required to construct one room in a building.

## Clarifying Questions

1. What is the size and shape of the room?
2. What type of bricks are being used?
3. What are the dimensions of one brick?
4. What is the height of the room?
5. What is the thickness of the walls?
6. What is the thickness of the mortar between bricks?
7. Should doors and windows be considered?
8. Should beams, foundation and reinforcement steel (sariya) be considered?
9. How many floors are there?
10. Should half or cut bricks be considered?

## Final Question

> Estimate the number of normal red bricks required to construct the four walls of one rectangular 10 ft × 10 ft room, with one floor, no doors or windows, standard mortar joints, and only full bricks considered.

## Solution

### Key Logic

Use a **volume-based approach**:

**Number of Bricks = Total Wall Volume ÷ Effective Volume of One Brick**

### Assumptions

| Parameter | Assumption |
|---|---:|
| Room shape | Rectangular |
| Room length | 10 ft |
| Room width | 10 ft |
| Room height | 10 ft |
| Wall thickness | 5 inches |
| Brick type | Normal red clay brick |
| Brick dimensions | 7.5 × 3.5 × 3.5 inches |
| Mortar joint | ~0.5 inch |
| Doors/windows | None |
| Beams/foundation | Not considered |
| Sariya | Not considered |
| Floors | 1 |
| Cut/half bricks | Not considered |
| Wastage | Not considered |

### Step 1: Calculate the Perimeter

**Perimeter = 2 × (Length + Width)**

= 2 × (10 + 10)

= **40 ft**

### Step 2: Calculate Total Wall Volume

Wall thickness:

5 inches = 5/12 ft

≈ **0.42 ft**

Therefore:

**Wall Volume = Perimeter × Height × Wall Thickness**

= 40 × 10 × 0.42

≈ **168 ft³**

### Step 3: Calculate Effective Volume of One Brick

Brick size:

**7.5 × 3.5 × 3.5 inches**

Including the mortar joint, use an effective brick size of approximately:

**8 × 4 × 4 inches**

Converting to feet:

- 8 inches = 0.667 ft
- 4 inches = 0.333 ft
- 4 inches = 0.333 ft

**Effective Brick Volume**

= 0.667 × 0.333 × 0.333

≈ **0.074 ft³**

### Step 4: Calculate Number of Bricks

**Number of Bricks = Total Wall Volume ÷ Effective Brick Volume**

= 168 ÷ 0.074

≈ **2,270 bricks**

Rounded:

## **Final Estimate: ~2,300 Bricks**

### Sanity Check

Approximately 13–14 bricks are required per cubic foot of brickwork.

**168 × 13.5 ≈ 2,270 bricks**

This is consistent with the volume-based calculation.

## Final Answer

> **Approximately 2,300 normal red bricks are required to construct the four walls of one 10 ft × 10 ft room.**
